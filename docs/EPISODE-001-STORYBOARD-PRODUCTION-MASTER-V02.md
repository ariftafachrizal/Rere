## 0. PURPOSE

Dokumen ini adalah **blueprint visual dan temporal utama** untuk produksi Episode 001 menggunakan image/video generation AI.

Storyboard ini bukan sekadar daftar gambar. Setiap shot harus menjawab:

1. Apa yang sedang terjadi?
2. Apa yang harus dilihat anak?
3. Apa yang harus dilakukan Rere?
4. Apa yang harus dikatakan Rere?
5. Kapan anak mendapat giliran merespons?
6. Bagaimana kamera membantu anak memahami action?
7. Bagaimana shot ini tersambung dengan shot sebelumnya dan sesudahnya?
8. Apa yang harus diberikan kepada Gemini agar visual/video dapat dibuat tanpa improvisasi identity atau story?

**Production principle:**

> **Storyboard defines the intended experience. Gemini renders the experience. Human review decides whether the render is acceptable.**

# 2. VISUAL EXPERIENCE TARGET

The episode should feel like:

> **Rere is genuinely playing and discovering colors with one child.**

It should NOT feel like:

- classroom lecture
- game show
- commercial
- hyperactive children's content
- random AI animation montage
- cinematic short film for adults

### Visual priority

**Rere → learning object → child response opportunity → environment**

Background exists to support the story, not compete with it.

---

# 3. GLOBAL SHOT LANGUAGE

### Framing

Default progression:

**Establish → Rere → Object → Interaction → Child-facing question → Hold/Pause → Response → Reaction**

### Camera

- child-height perspective
- mild natural perspective
- gentle movement only when motivated
- no extreme wide-angle distortion
- no dutch angle
- no handheld shake
- no whip pan
- no rapid zoom
- no unnecessary camera choreography

### Motion

Motion intensity scale:

- 0 = almost still
- 1 = micro movement / breathing / eye movement
- 2 = normal child movement
- 3 = energetic movement
- 4 = large action

Default for this episode: **1–2**.

### Lighting

Canonical playroom daytime lighting:

- soft warm daylight
- diffused key
- gentle fill
- soft contact shadows
- stable exposure
- accurate learning-object colors

### Composition

During learning shots:

- one primary learning object
- large enough to identify immediately
- strong separation from background
- Rere does not cover the object
- hands must remain visible during interaction
- sufficient negative space around learning object

---

# 4. MASTER CONTINUITY MAP

The entire episode takes place in the same playroom during the same daytime period.

### Spatial anchor

Use the canonical playroom layout as the geographic truth.

Recommended screen geography:

- learning table: central/right midground
- bookshelf: background
- toy storage: background/side
- play rug: foreground/midground
- window: background with consistent daylight direction
- Bunny: recurring background anchor, unless it would distract from learning

### Rere continuity

When possible:

- preserve screen direction
- preserve object hand
- preserve gaze direction
- preserve standing/sitting state unless action motivates change
- preserve object state
- preserve emotional state

### Learning object continuity

Each color object must have a stable identity:

| Color | Object | Required visual identity |
|---|---|---|
| Merah | Apple | unmistakably red, simple whole apple |
| Kuning | Banana | unmistakably yellow, simple curved banana |
| Biru | Ball | unmistakably blue, simple round preschool ball |
| Hijau | Plant/leaf | unmistakably green, simple healthy leaf/plant |
| Pink | Flower | unmistakably pink, simple friendly flower |

Do not recolor objects between shots.

# LEARNING SEQUENCE

The five color shots use the same basic learning rhythm:

**NOTICE → POINT/TOUCH → QUESTION → PAUSE → REVEAL → IMITATE → REWARD**

The answer must never be visually leaked during the question/pause.

## S04 — RED / APPLE

**Time:** 0:31–0:48  
**Duration:** 17 sec  
**Framing:** Medium → object CU → MCU Rere  
**Camera:** `CAM-LEARN-DISCOVERY-01`  
**Motion:** Level 1–2

### Learning objective
Child recognizes and attempts to say **merah**.

### Initial composition
Rere and several neutral/supporting playroom elements visible. Red apple is discoverable but not immediately presented as the answer.

### Action chain
1. Rere's gaze notices apple.
2. Head turns slightly.
3. Rere approaches/leans toward apple.
4. Rere picks up apple naturally.
5. Rere holds apple at chest level.
6. Rere looks at apple.
7. Rere looks at camera.
8. Question.
9. PAUSE with apple held still.
10. Answer.
11. Smile and imitation invitation.

### Dialogue
> “Wah! Rere menemukan sesuatu! Ini apel. Warnanya apa, ya?”

**PAUSE: 4 sec**

> “MERAH!”

> “Ayo bilang bersama Rere... me-rah!”

### Critical visual rule
During the question and 4-second pause, Rere must **not point directly at the apple as the answer**. She may hold the apple neutrally while looking between the object and camera.

### Object specification
Simple whole preschool-friendly apple, clear red surface, small natural stem, no bite, no text, no face.

### Camera
Start medium to show discovery. Move to close-up only enough to make apple color unmistakable. Return attention to Rere for answer/reaction.

### Audio
Music ducks during question and pause. No SFX during the answer word. Soft reward chime immediately after response window.

### Gemini instruction
Create a warm preschool learning scene in the canonical Rere playroom. Use the canonical Rere reference and an approved/simple red apple reference. Rere naturally discovers the apple, gently picks it up, holds it clearly at chest height, and looks toward camera with a curious expression before asking the color question. During the waiting moment, keep Rere relatively still and do not use a pointing gesture that reveals the answer. The apple must be unmistakably red, large enough to read, anatomically/physically believable in Rere's hand, and visually separated from the background.

### Reject if
- apple color is ambiguous
- Rere points at answer before pause
- apple changes shape between frames
- apple floats/clips through hand
- extra apples appear
- background contains competing red objects

# S09 — MINI QUIZ / CHILD TURN

**Time:** 1:57–2:30  
**Duration:** ~33 sec  
**Framing:** Medium learning composition  
**Camera:** `CAM-QUIZ-CHOICE-01`  
**Motion:** Level 0–1

## Purpose
Move from recognition to active recall. The child now gets the clearest turn to answer.

### Critical rule
The quiz must never become a speed test. Each question gets enough quiet time for a 2–4-year-old to inspect the options.

### Composition
Three large objects arranged with clear spacing. Rere is visible but positioned so her body does not block choices.

### Quiz A — RED

Objects: red apple + two non-red distractors from previously learned colors.

Dialogue:
> “Mana yang warna MERAH?”

**PAUSE: 4 sec**

Rere response:
> “Betul!”

### Quiz B — YELLOW

Objects: yellow banana + two non-yellow distractors.

Dialogue:
> “Mana yang warna KUNING?”

**PAUSE: 4 sec**

Rere response:
> “Betul!”

### Quiz C — PINK

Objects: pink flower + two non-pink distractors.

Dialogue:
> “Yang mana warna PINK?”

**PAUSE: 4 sec**

Rere response:
> “Yeay! Betul!”

### Rere acting
Before each question: neutral/open-palm presentation.  
During pause: still, curious, no answer leakage.  
After pause: smile + small celebration.

### Editing
Do not cut during the pause. Do not add animated highlights before the child response. Correct-answer highlight, if used, appears only after the response window.

### Audio
Music significantly reduced during each question and pause. Reward cue follows the pause.

### Gemini instruction
Create a clean preschool quiz composition in the canonical playroom. Rere sits/stands beside three large, clearly separated familiar objects. For each quiz variation, only one object is the correct answer. Keep object colors unambiguous, objects large enough for a 2–4-year-old to identify, and Rere's body positioned so she does not block the choices. Rere uses a neutral open-palm presentation gesture and does not point to the correct answer before the response window. No text is generated inside the image; labels/subtitles are added editorially.

### Reject if
- two answers could reasonably be correct
- object is too small
- Rere blocks choices
- color lighting changes between options
- decorative props create unintended alternatives
- pre-answer highlight reveals answer

# S10 — CREATE / DRAWING

**Time:** 2:30–3:05  
**Duration:** ~35 sec  
**Framing:** Over-Shoulder → Medium → Drawing CU  
**Camera:** `CAM-CREATE-ACTIVITY-01`  
**Motion:** Level 1–2

### Purpose
Connect recognition to active creation.

### Learning chain
**Choose → use → name → create → show**

### Action sequence
1. Rere returns to Learning Book.
2. Opens to blank page.
3. Selects red crayon.
4. Draws a simple element.
5. Selects yellow.
6. Adds another element.
7. Adds blue, green and pink elements.
8. Stops drawing.
9. Lifts/showcases the page.

### Dialogue
> “Sekarang, yuk kita pakai warna-warna ini untuk menggambar!”

While drawing:
> “Merah... kuning... biru... hijau... dan pink.”

### Drawing design
Keep drawing simple and child-readable. Suggested picture: simple rainbow/flower/house composition, but avoid adding unnecessary learning concepts. The five target colors must be visibly represented.

### Hand interaction
Crayon must remain correctly seated in Rere's hand. Drawing movement should contact paper; no floating crayon strokes.

### Camera
OTS shot establishes physical action. Medium shot shows Rere's engagement. Close-up shows the page and five colors. Final reveal returns to Rere.

### Gemini instruction
Generate a canonical Rere art activity in the established playroom. Rere uses the canonical Crayon Kit and Learning Book. Show believable hand-to-crayon-to-paper interaction while she creates a simple colorful preschool drawing. Use all five target colors visibly: red, yellow, blue, green and pink. Keep the drawing simple and readable. Do not generate written words or letters unless specifically requested; the learning is color use, not literacy.

### Reject if
- crayon floats
- drawing changes between shots without action
- color strokes do not touch paper
- page contains random text
- colors are muddy/indistinguishable
- Rere's hand anatomy fails

# 7. IMAGE GENERATION VS VIDEO GENERATION

Gemini may be used in two production modes.

## A. Image-first workflow

Use when:

- shot is mostly static
- exact composition matters more than complex motion
- image will become a still insert, transition, or reference
- image-to-video will provide controlled motion later

Recommended examples:

- S01 keyframe
- S03 CTA keyframe
- S04 learning keyframe
- S07 plant learning keyframe
- S09 quiz composition
- S11 achievement frame
- S12 closing keyframe

## B. Video generation workflow

Use when:

- physical interaction is important
- continuity of action matters
- movement communicates the learning

Recommended examples:

- S02 walking to table/opening book
- S04 picking up apple
- S05 reaching for banana
- S06 rolling/stopping ball
- S10 drawing
- S12 heart → wave

### Important
Do not ask a video model to invent the entire scene and action simultaneously when a controlled image/keyframe workflow can reduce drift.

Preferred pipeline:

**Canonical References → Keyframe → Video Motion → Human Review**

# 12. CHILD-CENTERED REVIEW QUESTIONS

Before approving the final edit, watch the episode once without reading the script and ask:

### Comprehension
- Can a 2–4-year-old tell what Rere wants to do?
- Is the learning object obvious?
- Is the question understandable from context?

### Participation
- Is there enough quiet time to answer?
- Does the visual stay stable during PAUSE?
- Does Rere avoid accidentally giving away the answer?

### Emotional experience
- Does Rere feel like a friend/companion?
- Is the energy warm rather than frantic?
- Does success feel encouraging rather than competitive?

### Visual quality
- Is Rere consistent?
- Is the playroom consistent?
- Are the colors accurate?
- Are hands and object interactions believable?

### Parent trust
- Is the educational purpose obvious?
- Is the CTA brief?
- Is anything manipulative, unsafe or overstimulating?
