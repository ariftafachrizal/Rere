# RERE — EDITING & MOTION BIBLE

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Production Standard  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Project:** YouTube Rere  
**Primary Audience:** Ages 2–4 (first 6 months)

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines how canonical Rere assets are transformed into finished video.

It controls:
- pacing
- shot duration
- cuts
- transitions
- character motion
- object motion
- text-on-screen
- subtitles
- learning pauses
- scene rhythm
- animation intensity
- retention without overstimulation

Core principle:

> **Editing should make the learning easier to follow, not make the video busier.**

The editing system follows:

> **PLAY → EXPERIENCE → IMITATE → LEARN**

---

## 2. PRODUCTION HIERARCHY

The finished scene must respect:

**Identity → Face → Anatomy → Outfit → Gesture/Expression → World → Props → Camera → Lighting → Audio → Editing/Motion → Scene Details**

Editing may arrange and emphasize approved elements, but must not redefine canonical identity.

---

## 3. EDITING PHILOSOPHY

Rere is designed for preschool children, not short-form adult attention patterns.

The edit should feel:
- clear
- calm enough to process
- playful
- predictable
- rewarding
- visually understandable
- emotionally safe

Avoid editing purely to maximize the number of cuts.

A child should be able to understand:
1. where Rere is
2. what Rere is looking at
3. what Rere is doing
4. what Rere is asking
5. when it is their turn
6. what the answer/action is
7. why the result matters

---

## 4. CANONICAL LEARNING RHYTHM

Use the recurring pattern:

**Story → Discovery → Question → PAUSE → Answer → Reward → Repeat**

Editing must make each phase visually and sonically distinct enough to understand.

### Example

**Discovery:** medium shot of Rere finding a red object.  
**Question:** closer framing of Rere + object.  
**PAUSE:** hold composition; reduce motion.  
**Answer:** reveal/highlight object.  
**Reward:** Rere reacts + short reward cue.  
**Repeat:** move naturally to the next example.

---

## 5. SCENE RHYTHM

Every scene should have a clear purpose.

Recommended internal structure:

1. Establish
2. Enter / orient
3. Discover
4. Interact
5. Ask
6. Pause
7. Reveal / answer
8. React / reward
9. Transition

Not every scene requires all nine steps, but the editor should know which function is being served.

---

## 6. SHOT DURATION GUIDANCE

Shot duration is flexible and depends on action complexity.

General starting points for ages 2–4:

| Shot purpose | Starting range |
|---|---:|
| Establishing | 3–6 sec |
| Simple interaction | 2–5 sec |
| Learning focus | 3–6 sec |
| Question | 2–4 sec before PAUSE |
| PAUSE | typically 2–5+ sec |
| Answer/reveal | 2–5 sec |
| Emotional reaction | 2–4 sec |
| Transition | 0.5–1.5 sec |

These are **starting ranges, not hard limits**.

The correct duration is the shortest duration that still allows a preschool child to perceive, process, and participate.

---

## 7. PAUSE IS A FIRST-CLASS EDITING ELEMENT

The PAUSE is one of Rere's most important production tools.

During PAUSE:
- avoid unnecessary cuts
- avoid camera shake
- avoid rapid character movement
- reduce music density
- avoid decorative text changes
- keep the learning object visible
- preserve Rere's readable expression

The child must feel:

> **“Sekarang giliranku.”**

Do not prematurely reveal the answer.

---

## 8. ANSWER / REWARD TIMING

After PAUSE:

**Child response → short processing beat → Rere confirmation → visual/SFX reward**

Do not stack every reward simultaneously at maximum intensity.

A simple correct-answer moment may need only:
- Rere smile
- small movement
- short sonic cue

---

## 9. CUTTING RULES

### Cut when:
- location changes
- story beat changes
- learning object needs focus
- action needs a clearer angle
- emotional beat changes
- repetition needs controlled variation

### Do not cut merely because:
- the shot has lasted a few seconds
- a generic editing template expects a cut
- the editor wants more visual energy

### Avoid:
- random jump cuts
- excessive reaction inserts
- rapid alternation between face and object
- cutting during the key word
- cutting before a child can identify the object

---

## 10. TRANSITION SYSTEM

Default transition:

**Straight cut.**

Use gentle transitions only when they serve a narrative purpose.

Preferred:
- straight cut
- soft dissolve for emotional/time transition
- gentle wipe for playful movement
- simple musical transition
- visual movement motivated by Rere's action

Avoid:
- flashy preset packs
- glitch transitions
- aggressive zoom transitions
- spinning transitions
- strobe effects
- frequent particle explosions

Transition identity must remain compatible with the Camera and Audio Bibles.

---

## 11. MOTION LANGUAGE

Rere's movement should be:
- soft
- rounded
- readable
- expressive
- slightly exaggerated for clarity
- physically plausible within the stylized 3D world

Movement hierarchy:

**Intent → body movement → hand gesture → facial reaction → secondary motion**

Do not animate every body part independently just to create activity.

---

## 12. CHARACTER MOTION

Canonical Rere actions include:
- walk
- stop
- look
- point
- wave
- reach
- pick up
- hold
- open
- close
- place
- sort
- count
- compare
- share
- clap gently
- form heart with hands

Movement should begin from a readable intention.

Example:

**Look at object → notice → smile → reach → pick up.**

Avoid teleporting directly from neutral pose to completed interaction.

---

## 13. MOTION ANTICIPATION

Important actions should have a small anticipation beat.

Example:

**See ball → prepare → reach → pick up.**

Anticipation helps preschool viewers understand cause and effect.

Do not make anticipation so long that it becomes tedious.

---

## 14. FOLLOW-THROUGH / SECONDARY MOTION

Use subtle secondary motion for:
- ponytail
- bow
- clothing
- backpack
- soft toys
- lightweight props

Secondary motion should support physical readability.

Avoid excessive bouncing, spinning, or elastic deformation.

---

## 15. FACIAL ANIMATION

Facial expression is a learning and storytelling signal.

Required emotional readability:
- neutral
- happy
- curious
- surprised
- thinking
- proud
- gentle concern
- calm

Expression changes should be readable without becoming exaggerated caricature.

Protect canonical:
- eye shape
- eyebrow relationship
- nose
- mouth/lip shape
- eyelashes
- facial proportions

Motion must never change Rere's identity.

---

## 16. EYE DIRECTION

Eye direction is a major attention tool.

When introducing a learning object:

**Rere looks → camera/object emphasis → child understands target.**

Rere should usually look at the object she is discussing or interacting with.

Avoid gaze that contradicts the spoken instruction.

---

## 17. HAND / OBJECT INTERACTION

Object interactions must be visually causal.

Use:

**Reach → contact → grip → move → release.**

For learning:

**SEE → TOUCH → NAME → COMPARE → ACT → REPEAT**

This should be consistent with the Prop Bible.

Do not let objects float, clip through hands, change scale unexpectedly, or magically move without narrative reason.

---

## 18. RECURRING PROP CONTINUITY

Canonical props must not drift between shots.

Preserve:
- silhouette
- scale
- color relationships
- material
- markings
- accessories
- interaction behavior

Editing must not hide a continuity error by rapidly cutting around it; production should fix the source asset whenever practical.

---

## 19. CAMERA + EDITING

The Camera Bible controls how attention is framed.

The Editing Bible controls when that framing changes.

Use:

**Establish → Enter → Interact → Focus → Pause → Reveal → Exit.**

Avoid excessive switching between wide and close shots without narrative reason.

---

## 20. DIGITAL ZOOM / CROP

Digital push-ins may be used sparingly when the source image/video supports them.

Use for:
- learning-object emphasis
- gentle emotional emphasis
- transition into a question

Avoid:
- constant Ken Burns movement
- aggressive punch-ins
- repeated face zooms
- zooming on every keyword

A zoom should communicate **why the viewer should look here**.

---

## 21. TEXT-ON-SCREEN

Text is supporting material, not the primary teaching channel for ages 2–4.

Use text for:
- single word labels
- color names
- simple numbers
- simple shape names
- occasional bilingual vocabulary
- chapter/episode identification

Prefer:
- one concept at a time
- large readable type
- high contrast
- short words
- generous spacing

Avoid paragraphs and dense educational slides.

---

## 22. TEXT TIMING

Text should appear when the corresponding concept is introduced.

Pattern:

**Object appears → Rere names it → label appears → label remains long enough to recognize → label exits.**

Do not flash words rapidly.

Do not introduce text before the child has a visual referent unless there is a deliberate learning reason.

---

## 23. SUBTITLE SYSTEM

Subtitles should improve accessibility without overwhelming the frame.

Rules:
- synchronize closely with speech
- use short readable chunks
- preserve important spoken words
- avoid covering Rere's face or learning object
- use consistent placement
- maintain the same subtitle style across episodes

For very young viewers, subtitles are a support layer, not a requirement for every learning interaction.

---

## 24. ON-SCREEN LEARNING HIGHLIGHTS

When emphasizing a target concept:
- use subtle scale change
- gentle glow/highlight
- pointing gesture
- framing
- simple label
- controlled motion

Do not combine every technique at once.

One strong cue is usually better than five competing cues.

---

## 25. REPETITION WITHOUT MONOTONY

Repetition is essential for early learning.

Use controlled variation:

### Repeat 1
Rere demonstrates.

### Repeat 2
Rere asks the child.

### Repeat 3
Child identifies/acts.

### Repeat 4
Rere applies the concept in a new context.

The concept remains stable while the situation changes.

---

## 26. RETENTION WITHOUT OVERSTIMULATION

Do not copy the editing language of high-intensity short-form content.

Retention should come from:
- curiosity
- story progression
- participation
- repetition
- discovery
- emotional warmth
- anticipation
- clear rewards

Not from:
- constant cuts
- loud effects
- flashing colors
- rapid zooms
- artificial urgency
- fear
- surprise overload

---

## 27. MOTION INTENSITY LEVELS

### Level 0 — Still
For explanation, PAUSE, calm emotion.

### Level 1 — Gentle
Small gestures, breathing, blinking, subtle secondary motion.

### Level 2 — Playful
Walking, pointing, sorting, light bouncing.

### Level 3 — Energetic
Running, active play, exciting discovery.

### Level 4 — Special
Rarely used. Major story climax or imagination sequence.

Default for ages 2–4: **Level 1–2**.

Level 3 should have a clear narrative reason.

Level 4 must be exceptional.

---

## 28. MOTION TRANSITIONS

Prefer transitions motivated by physical action.

Examples:
- Rere walks past camera → next location
- Rere opens a book → next learning scene
- object moves into foreground → match cut
- page turn → new activity
- hand places object → next shot continues action

These transitions help children understand spatial and causal continuity.

---

## 29. MATCH CUTS

Match cuts may be used for learning relationships.

Examples:
- red crayon → red apple
- circle toy → round plate
- three blocks → three flowers

The visual relationship should be obvious.

Do not use complex symbolic editing that requires adult interpretation.

---

## 30. MONTAGE

Montage is appropriate for:
- collecting examples
- repeating vocabulary
- showing a sequence of creations
- review

Keep each item on screen long enough to identify.

For learning review, a slower montage is often better than a rapid montage.

---

## 31. REVIEW SECTION

Every appropriate learning episode should include a compact review.

Recommended:

**Recall → Question → PAUSE → Answer → Positive reinforcement.**

The review should reuse familiar visuals and language.

Do not introduce many new elements during the review.

---

## 32. INTRO EDITING LANGUAGE

Opening sequence should be consistent across episodes.

Recommended rhythm:

**Sonic logo → Rere visual → canonical opening → episode hook.**

Do not let the intro become unnecessarily long.

The child should reach the episode's actual learning/story content quickly.

---

## 33. CLOSING EDITING LANGUAGE

Closing should slow the pace slightly.

Sequence:

1. accomplishment
2. gratitude
3. heart gesture
4. “Belajar... Bermain... Berbagi... Bersama Rere!”
5. goodbye
6. end card

Avoid adding unrelated jokes, extra CTAs, or rapid end-screen elements after the canonical goodbye.

---

## 34. CTA EDITING

CTA is secondary to learning.

If included:
- keep it short
- place it naturally
- do not interrupt the key learning moment
- do not use manipulative urgency
- do not pressure children to engage

Example:

> “Kalau suka belajar bersama Rere, sampai jumpa di video berikutnya, ya!”

The learning experience remains the primary product.

---

## 35. AUDIO + EDITING

Editing must preserve the Audio Identity Bible hierarchy:

**Voice → important SFX → music → ambience.**

Never cut in a way that damages:
- a key word
- a question
- the PAUSE
- a child response opportunity
- a reward cue

Music transitions should support the cut rather than dictate it when doing so harms learning clarity.

---

## 36. LIGHTING + EDITING CONTINUITY

Do not create accidental emotional discontinuity through editing.

Examples to avoid:
- cheerful scene → dark dramatic shot → cheerful scene without narrative reason
- morning ambience → night lighting → morning within seconds
- warm learning moment interrupted by unrelated dramatic grade

Editing should respect the Lighting Bible's emotional and temporal continuity.

---

## 37. SCENE CONTINUITY

Maintain:
- screen direction
- spatial orientation
- prop placement
- Rere's hand/object state
- character position
- time of day
- lighting direction
- audio ambience

When continuity intentionally changes, provide a readable transition.

---

## 38. 180-DEGREE RULE

Respect the established spatial axis unless there is a deliberate transition.

For example:

**Rere left → object right**

should not suddenly become

**Rere right → object left**

without a readable reason.

This is especially important when children are learning relationships between objects.

---

## 39. SCREEN DIRECTION

When Rere walks or moves through a sequence, preserve direction where possible.

Consistent direction helps children build spatial understanding.

Use direction changes only when the story requires it and establish the new direction clearly.

---

## 40. LOOPABLE MICRO-ACTIONS

Small loops may be used for:
- blinking
- breathing
- gentle sway
- idle posture
- soft environmental movement

Loops must not look mechanical or hypnotic.

Avoid repeating an identical motion at an obviously fixed interval.

---

## 41. AI VIDEO / ANIMATION GENERATION ANCHOR

Use this anchor when generating motion:

> **Canonical Rere preschool animation, soft pastel 3D CGI/kawaii, warm child-safe world, natural readable preschool movement, gentle expressive gestures, clear cause-and-effect interactions, stable character anatomy and face, stable canonical outfit, consistent recurring props and environment, child-perspective cinematography, soft lighting, restrained motion, educational attention guidance, enough stillness for ages 2–4, no overstimulation, no identity drift.**

### Negative motion prompt

Avoid:
- hyperactive movement
- frantic camera
- rapid cuts
- jitter
- shaky cam
- body deformation
- face morphing
- identity drift
- changing hairstyle
- changing outfit without reason
- extra fingers/limbs
- floating objects
- object clipping
- teleporting props
- impossible physics
- rubbery anatomy
- excessive squash/stretch
- seizure-like flashing
- strobe
- glitch effects
- horror motion
- adult action-movie movement
- aggressive zooms
- random background movement

---

## 42. EDITING TEMPLATE — STANDARD LEARNING SCENE

Use as a starting template:

**A. Establish**  
2–5 sec. Show place and context.

**B. Discovery**  
2–5 sec. Rere notices target.

**C. Focus**  
2–4 sec. Camera/edit directs attention.

**D. Question**  
2–4 sec. Rere asks.

**E. PAUSE**  
2–5+ sec. Child participates.

**F. Answer**  
2–4 sec. Reveal/confirmation.

**G. Reward**  
1–3 sec. Rere reaction + subtle SFX.

**H. Repeat / progress**  
Move naturally to the next example.

These ranges can expand or contract based on complexity and child testing.

---

## 43. EDITING TEMPLATE — STORY SCENE

**Establish → action → reaction → consequence → next goal**

Story scenes should not feel like a sequence of disconnected educational cards.

Learning should emerge naturally from Rere's experience.

---

## 44. EDITING TEMPLATE — REVIEW

**Recall → Question → PAUSE → Answer → Reward → Final recap**

Use familiar visual assets whenever possible.

---

## 45. EPISODE PACING TARGET

For the first six months, episodes should generally favor:
- short scenes
- clear learning beats
- repeated participation
- low visual complexity per shot
- predictable structure

A 3–4 minute episode does not need dozens of visual ideas.

Depth and repetition are more valuable than constant novelty.

---

## 46. THUMBNAIL / HERO FRAME

The editor may select a visually strong frame for thumbnail use.

Preferred:
- Rere clearly visible
- one strong emotional expression
- one clear learning/story object
- simple background
- strong visual hierarchy
- minimal text

Do not select a frame with distorted motion or ambiguous object relationships.

---

## 47. QUALITY GATE — PRE-EDIT

Before editing:

- [ ] canonical Rere asset confirmed
- [ ] correct outfit confirmed
- [ ] correct environment confirmed
- [ ] correct props confirmed
- [ ] camera plan confirmed
- [ ] lighting state confirmed
- [ ] voice/audio assets confirmed
- [ ] scene objective defined

---

## 48. QUALITY GATE — EDIT

Before final export:

- [ ] story is understandable without rushing
- [ ] every cut has a purpose
- [ ] PAUSE is protected
- [ ] learning objects remain visible
- [ ] Rere's identity remains stable
- [ ] prop continuity is correct
- [ ] screen direction is consistent
- [ ] motion intensity is appropriate
- [ ] text is readable
- [ ] subtitles do not obstruct key visuals
- [ ] audio hierarchy is preserved
- [ ] no overstimulation
- [ ] transitions are restrained

---

## 49. QUALITY GATE — CHILD-CENTERED REVIEW

Ask:

1. Can a 2–4-year-old tell what Rere is doing?
2. Can they identify the learning object?
3. Is there enough time to answer?
4. Does the video clearly signal when it is their turn?
5. Is repetition understandable?
6. Are there distracting movements unrelated to the lesson?
7. Does the reward feel warm rather than overwhelming?
8. Does the scene remain emotionally safe?

If the answer to several questions is “no,” simplify the edit before adding more effects.

---

## 50. FINAL PRODUCTION PRINCIPLE

> **Clarity beats complexity. Participation beats spectacle. Repetition beats novelty. Warmth beats overstimulation.**

The goal is not to make every second visually exciting.

The goal is to make every important moment **understandable, memorable, and participatory**.

Rere should leave children feeling:

**“Aku tahu apa yang terjadi. Aku boleh mencoba. Aku berhasil belajar.”**
