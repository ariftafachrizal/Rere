# LIVE — Three-Year Developmental Progression

**Status:** Approved Working Framework — 2026-09-12

## Developmental Spine

> **NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → PRACTICE → PARTICIPATE → INDEPENDENTLY APPLY**

This is a curriculum reference, not a rigid age gate or formal assessment.

## Three-Year Arc

### Year 1 — Participate in Everyday Life
**NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → PRACTICE**

Children notice familiar routines, boundaries, hazards, and simple responsibilities; connect actions to immediate reasons; and practice with modeling and support.

### Year 2 — Contribute with Purpose
**PRACTICE → PARTICIPATE**

Children take part in longer routine sequences, simple responsibilities, shared-space care, turn-taking, basic safety choices, and helping others. They begin to explain simple reasons for actions.

### Year 3 — Apply & Take Increasing Responsibility
**PARTICIPATE → INDEPENDENTLY APPLY**

Children increasingly plan and complete familiar routines, adapt when plans change, recognize risks, contribute to group goals, and make safe/value-aligned choices while still receiving appropriate support.

## Area Progression

| Area | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Values & respect | notice/model | practice in interaction | apply/adapt in new contexts |
| Daily life skills | participate with support | complete sequences with support | increasingly independent routines |
| Safety | recognize simple hazards/rules | choose safe action in familiar contexts | recognize risk and adapt safely |
| Responsibility | small contribution | shared responsibility | simple planning/contribution |
| Agency | choose between safe options | choose method/order | plan and adapt within boundaries |
| Healthy habits | participate in routine | explain simple purpose | apply consistently across contexts |

## Complexity Model

Progress increases across:

- **Routine complexity:** single action → short sequence → multi-step routine
- **Context:** familiar → varied → less predictable/new
- **Independence:** model → supported practice → shared responsibility → independent application
- **Reasoning:** what to do → why it helps → choose/adapt what to do
- **Social responsibility:** self → shared space → contribution to group

## Spiral Examples

### Handwashing
- Year 1: notice sequence and participate with help.
- Year 2: follow the sequence and connect it to keeping hands clean before eating.
- Year 3: recognize when handwashing is needed in a new context and complete the routine with increasing independence.

### Tidying toys
- Year 1: put one or two items away with modeling.
- Year 2: help restore a shared play area and understand why.
- Year 3: plan a simple cleanup approach and adapt when there are more items.

### Road safety
- Year 1: recognize stop/wait and stay with trusted adult.
- Year 2: practice a predictable crossing sequence with adult support.
- Year 3: identify safer choices across varied road situations while understanding that young children still need adult supervision.

### Helping others
- Year 1: notice a simple need and participate in a modeled helping action.
- Year 2: choose a concrete way to help.
- Year 3: identify what contribution is needed, communicate, and follow through.

## What Progress Does NOT Mean

- perfect obedience;
- performing routines without adult supervision when safety requires it;
- never making a mess or mistake;
- memorizing rules without understanding;
- fear of consequences;
- forced affection or compliance;
- adult responsibilities being transferred to children;
- treating every family routine as universal.

## Design Implication

LIVE episodes should repeatedly place useful life skills, values, and safety decisions inside play and real-life-like contexts. Increasing difficulty should come from sequence, context, agency, and transfer—not from longer lectures or stricter demands.
