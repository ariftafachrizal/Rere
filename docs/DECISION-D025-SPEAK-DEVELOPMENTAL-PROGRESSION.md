# D-025 — SPEAK Three-Year Developmental Progression

**Status:** Approved Working Framework — 2026-09-12

## Decision

SPEAK uses the following developmental progression as its three-year curriculum reference:

> **NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT → COMMUNICATE**

The progression is developmental and conceptual, not a rigid age gate or formal assessment framework.

## Three-Year Arc

- **Year 1:** NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND — build communication foundation, comprehension, participation, and safe early English exposure.
- **Year 2:** RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT — use language for real purposes, functional communication, short exchanges, and cooperative play.
- **Year 3:** INTERACT → COMMUNICATE — sustain interaction, share ideas, narrate, explain simple reasons, negotiate choices, collaborate, and transfer language to new contexts.

## English Arc

The broader communication progression remains primary. English uses a parallel progression:

> **HEAR → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → USE → GENERALIZE**

Imitation remains a learning technique, not a developmental endpoint.

## Key Principles

1. Communication is the primary goal; English is an important language pathway, not the definition of SPEAK.
2. Comprehension can precede verbal production.
3. Nonverbal, home-language, mixed-language, and English responses can all be meaningful participation.
4. Progress is defined by increasing ability to communicate meaningfully, not by vocabulary count or English-only performance.
5. Complexity should grow through context, response, interaction, and language flexibility—not vocabulary volume alone.
6. Important communication functions should spiral across years with increasing agency and transfer.
7. Home language remains an asset throughout the curriculum.
