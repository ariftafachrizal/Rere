# RERE — CONTENT & EPISODE PRODUCTION BIBLE

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Production Standard  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Project:** YouTube Rere  
**Primary Audience:** Ages 2–4 (first 6 months)

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This document converts the Rere creative system into a repeatable episode-production pipeline.

It defines:

**Idea → Learning Objective → Story → Script → Storyboard → Asset Selection → Generation → Voice → Audio → Edit → QA → Packaging → Publish → Learn**

The goal is to make Rere scalable without sacrificing:
- character consistency
- educational quality
- child safety
- emotional warmth
- production efficiency
- parent trust

---

## 2. PRODUCTION PRINCIPLE

> **One episode, one clear learning promise, one coherent experience.**

An episode may contain multiple activities, but the child should be able to understand its central learning idea.

Do not add content merely because there is unused runtime.

---

## 3. LEARNING PHILOSOPHY

All episodes should support:

> **PLAY → EXPERIENCE → IMITATE → LEARN**

Core learning pillars:

- **DISCOVER**
- **SPEAK**
- **FEEL**
- **LIVE**
- **CREATE**

PLAY is the method, not a sixth pillar.

---

## 4. EPISODE DESIGN RULES

For the first six months:

- target ages 2–4
- prioritize familiar concepts
- use concrete objects
- use short sentences
- repeat important vocabulary
- include child participation
- provide visible cause and effect
- keep emotional stakes safe
- avoid excessive information density

The episode should feel like a play experience that happens to teach something meaningful.

---

## 5. EPISODE OBJECTIVE CARD

Every episode starts with an objective card.

Required fields:

- Episode ID
- Working title
- Target age
- Primary learning pillar
- Secondary pillar(s)
- Learning objective
- Key vocabulary
- Expected child action
- Core story premise
- Primary world/location
- Primary props
- Approximate duration
- Episode status

Example:

```text
Episode ID: RERE-EP-001
Working title: Rere Belajar Warna
Target age: 2–4
Primary pillar: DISCOVER
Learning objective: mengenali lima warna dasar melalui benda familiar
Key vocabulary: merah, kuning, biru, hijau, pink
Expected action: menunjuk / menyebutkan warna
World: Playroom
Primary props: Crayon Kit, Learning Book
Duration: 3–4 minutes
```

---

## 6. LEARNING OBJECTIVE QUALITY GATE

A good objective should be:

- observable
- simple
- age-appropriate
- achievable in one episode
- repeatable
- connected to real-world experience

Prefer:

> “Anak dapat mengenali warna merah, kuning, dan biru pada benda familiar.”

Avoid:

> “Anak memahami konsep teori warna secara komprehensif.”

The first is observable and appropriate; the second is not.

---

## 7. CONTENT PILLAR SELECTION

Choose one primary pillar.

### DISCOVER
For observing, identifying, comparing, exploring.

### SPEAK
For vocabulary, naming, listening, simple expression.

### FEEL
For emotions, empathy, confidence, relationships.

### LIVE
For habits, routines, safety, independence, daily life.

### CREATE
For drawing, building, making, experimenting, imaginative expression.

A secondary pillar may support the primary one.

---

## 8. TOPIC SELECTION

Prioritize topics with:

1. high everyday familiarity
2. strong visual representation
3. simple vocabulary
4. natural opportunities for participation
5. repeatability
6. safe emotional context
7. potential for multiple episodes

Examples:
- colors
- shapes
- numbers
- animals
- food
- body parts
- family
- feelings
- hygiene
- sharing
- routines
- nature
- vehicles
- simple sorting

---

## 9. TOPIC CLUSTERING

Do not exhaust an educational topic in one episode.

Example:

**Colors cluster**
- primary color recognition
- color hunt
- matching colors
- sorting colors
- colors in nature
- mixing basic colors later

This creates a learning progression and reusable production system.

---

## 10. EPISODE PROGRESSION

A simple progression model:

### Level 1 — Recognize
“Ini warna apa?”

### Level 2 — Match
“Mana yang sama?”

### Level 3 — Compare
“Yang mana lebih besar?”

### Level 4 — Act
“Yuk ambil yang merah.”

### Level 5 — Apply
“Coba cari benda merah di sekitar kamu.”

Episodes should not jump too quickly to higher complexity.

---

## 11. STORY-FIRST EDUCATIONAL DESIGN

Whenever practical, embed the lesson in a story.

Instead of:

> “Hari ini kita akan belajar lima warna.”

Prefer a motivation such as:

> “Rere mau menggambar. Wah, krayonnya banyak sekali! Yuk cari warna yang Rere butuhkan.”

The learning objective remains clear, but the child experiences a reason to learn.

---

## 12. STANDARD EPISODE ARCHITECTURE

Use the established architecture:

1. Intro
2. Hook / theme
3. Short CTA if appropriate
4. Content / activity
5. Closing

Within the content, use:

**Story → Discovery → Question → PAUSE → Answer → Reward → Repeat**

---

## 13. OPENING LOCK

Standard opening:

> **“Hai teman-teman, aku Rere, main dan belajar bersama Rere, yuk!”**

The opening should remain consistent across episodes.

The episode-specific hook follows quickly so the intro does not become a barrier to learning.

---

## 14. HOOK DESIGN

The hook should introduce a simple question, goal, problem, or discovery.

Good hooks:
- Rere needs to find something
- Rere notices something unusual
- Rere wants to make something
- Rere is preparing for an activity
- Rere discovers a new object

Avoid:
- fear bait
- artificial danger
- screaming
- exaggerated emergencies
- confusing mystery with no payoff

---

## 15. CTA RULE

CTA is optional and secondary.

If included, keep it brief and natural.

Never interrupt:
- the opening learning hook
- a question
- PAUSE
- a key explanation

Do not pressure children into engagement.

---

## 16. SCRIPTING SYSTEM

Write scripts for spoken delivery, not essays.

Each dialogue line should have:
- speaker
- exact wording
- action
- emotion
- learning purpose
- PAUSE marker when needed

Example:

```text
RERE:
“Hmm... yang mana warna merah?”

[ACTION: Rere looks at three crayons.]

[PAUSE: 3–5 sec]

RERE:
“Yuk, kita cari bersama!”
```

---

## 17. LANGUAGE RULES

For ages 2–4:

Prefer:
- short sentences
- concrete words
- repetition
- familiar verbs
- simple questions
- direct instructions

Avoid:
- long explanations
- abstract terminology
- multiple instructions in one sentence
- unnecessary baby talk
- sarcastic language

Rere should speak naturally, warmly, and clearly.

---

## 18. KEYWORD REPETITION

Important learning words should appear multiple times in meaningful contexts.

Example:

**merah → merah → benda merah → cari merah → ulang merah**

Do not repeat mechanically without changing context.

---

## 19. CHILD PARTICIPATION

Every appropriate learning episode should provide opportunities to:

- look
- point
- name
- count
- choose
- match
- sort
- imitate
- move
- answer

The child should not only watch Rere perform.

---

## 20. PAUSE DESIGN

Every child question should be evaluated for a deliberate PAUSE.

During PAUSE:
- keep target object visible
- keep composition stable
- reduce competing motion
- reduce music density
- allow processing time

The PAUSE duration should reflect task difficulty.

Simple recognition may need less time than a multi-step action.

---

## 21. STORYBOARD SYSTEM

Storyboard each scene with:

- scene number
- purpose
- location
- character state
- prop state
- camera shot
- action
- dialogue
- learning objective
- audio/SFX
- lighting state
- transition
- continuity notes

Recommended columns:

| Scene | Purpose | Visual | Dialogue | Learning | Audio | Camera | Transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

---

## 22. ASSET SELECTION BEFORE GENERATION

Do not generate scenes before identifying canonical assets.

Select:

1. canonical Rere character
2. correct outfit
3. canonical gesture/expression reference
4. canonical world
5. canonical props
6. camera preset
7. lighting preset
8. audio direction

Only then generate the scene.

---

## 23. CANONICAL ASSET RULE

A generated scene is not allowed to redefine a canonical asset.

If the scene generation produces a different:
- face
- hair
- anatomy
- outfit
- prop
- world architecture

fix the generation rather than accepting the drift as a new design.

Canonical master assets remain the source of truth.

---

## 24. MASTER → DERIVATIVE WORKFLOW

For recurring elements:

**Canonical Master → Derivative Asset → Scene Use**

Never:

**Canonical Master → unrelated regeneration → new visual identity**

This is especially important for:
- Rere
- Pink Bunny
- Backpack
- Learning Book
- recurring environments

---

## 25. SCENE GENERATION WORKFLOW

For AI-generated scenes:

1. read relevant Bibles
2. select canonical references
3. define scene objective
4. define action
5. define camera
6. define lighting
7. define audio intention
8. generate
9. inspect continuity
10. reject or revise if canonical drift occurs
11. approve scene
12. version asset

---

## 26. GEMINI PRODUCTION RULE

Gemini is treated as a downstream production engine.

It receives controlled inputs from the Rere system rather than inventing the system itself.

Input priority:

**Canonical identity → scene objective → canonical assets → camera → lighting → motion → audio → scene details**

Human review remains required.

---

## 27. GENERATION PROMPT STRUCTURE

Recommended prompt order:

1. project identity
2. character identity
3. environment identity
4. prop identity
5. scene objective
6. character action
7. camera
8. lighting
9. motion
10. audio intent if supported
11. style constraints
12. continuity constraints
13. negative prompt

This order reduces accidental reinterpretation of core identity.

---

## 28. SCENE ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

A generated scene passes only if:

### Identity
- [ ] Rere matches canonical reference
- [ ] face is stable
- [ ] anatomy is stable
- [ ] outfit is correct

### Environment
- [ ] correct world
- [ ] architecture is consistent
- [ ] scale is correct

### Props
- [ ] correct canonical props
- [ ] no drift
- [ ] interaction is physically readable

### Camera
- [ ] intended shot
- [ ] focal point is clear
- [ ] child perspective is appropriate

### Lighting
- [ ] correct emotional/time-of-day state
- [ ] skin tone preserved
- [ ] learning object colors preserved

### Motion
- [ ] action is readable
- [ ] no deformation
- [ ] no jitter
- [ ] no teleportation

### Learning
- [ ] learning objective is visually understandable
- [ ] child participation is possible

Reject if critical criteria fail.

---

## 29. VOICE PRODUCTION

Voice production follows the Voice Bible.

Workflow:

**Script lock → voice generation/recording → pronunciation review → timing review → emotional review → approve → version**

Do not edit the animation around an unapproved voice if the dialogue timing is likely to change significantly.

---

## 30. AUDIO PRODUCTION

Follow the Audio Identity Bible.

Workflow:

**Voice → music → SFX → ambience → mix → QA**

The voice remains the priority layer.

---

## 31. EDITING PRODUCTION

Follow the Editing & Motion Bible.

Workflow:

**Approved scene assets → rough assembly → timing → PAUSE → transitions → text/subtitles → audio mix → polish → QA**

Do not add effects simply because the timeline feels empty.

---

## 32. EPISODE ASSEMBLY

Recommended order:

1. lock voice/dialogue
2. assemble story structure
3. place canonical scenes
4. align actions to dialogue
5. place PAUSE moments
6. add learning text
7. add music
8. add SFX
9. add ambience
10. refine cuts/transitions
11. perform continuity review
12. export review version

---

## 33. INTERNAL REVIEW PASSES

Use separate passes rather than trying to judge everything at once.

### Pass 1 — Story
Does it make sense?

### Pass 2 — Learning
Is the objective actually taught?

### Pass 3 — Character
Does Rere remain Rere?

### Pass 4 — Continuity
Do world, props, camera, lighting, and actions match?

### Pass 5 — Audio
Is dialogue clear and audio hierarchy correct?

### Pass 6 — Editing
Is pacing appropriate?

### Pass 7 — Child-centered
Can a 2–4-year-old follow and participate?

### Pass 8 — Packaging
Does title/thumbnail honestly represent the episode?

---

## 34. CHILD-CENTERED REVIEW

Ask:

1. What is the child supposed to notice first?
2. Is that visually obvious?
3. What is the child supposed to say or do?
4. Is there enough time?
5. Does Rere invite participation naturally?
6. Is the reward clear?
7. Is the repetition useful?
8. Is anything visually or sonically distracting?
9. Would a parent understand the educational purpose?

If the answer is unclear, simplify.

---

## 35. PARENT REVIEW

A parent/caregiver should quickly understand:
- what the episode is about
- what the child may learn
- whether the tone is safe
- whether the content is age-appropriate

Trust is part of product quality.

---

## 36. VERSIONING

Every production stage should be versioned.

Suggested:

`RERE-EP-001-SCRIPT-V01`  
`RERE-EP-001-STORYBOARD-V01`  
`RERE-EP-001-SCENE-03-V02`  
`RERE-EP-001-VOICE-V01`  
`RERE-EP-001-AUDIO-V01`  
`RERE-EP-001-EDIT-V01`  
`RERE-EP-001-PACKAGING-V01`

Approved versions should never be silently replaced.

---

## 37. EPISODE STATUS SYSTEM

Recommended lifecycle:

`IDEA`
→ `OBJECTIVE LOCKED`
→ `SCRIPT`
→ `STORYBOARD`
→ `ASSET READY`
→ `SCENE GENERATION`
→ `VOICE`
→ `AUDIO`
→ `EDIT`
→ `QA`
→ `PACKAGING`
→ `READY TO PUBLISH`
→ `PUBLISHED`
→ `LEARNING REVIEW`

Only move forward when the required gate is passed.

---

## 38. STOP / REJECT CONDITIONS

Stop production and fix the source when:

- Rere identity drifts
- recurring prop changes design
- world architecture changes unexpectedly
- learning objective becomes unclear
- voice becomes inconsistent
- child participation disappears
- pacing becomes overstimulating
- title/thumbnail becomes misleading
- continuity errors are visible
- AI introduces unsafe content

Do not normalize defects simply because fixing them costs another generation.

---

## 39. PRODUCTION EFFICIENCY

Scale should come from reusable systems.

Reuse:
- canonical character assets
- canonical outfits
- canonical gestures
- canonical worlds
- canonical props
- camera presets
- lighting presets
- voice identity
- sonic identity
- editing templates
- packaging templates

Do not rebuild the same system for every episode.

## 42. BATCH PRODUCTION

Once the system is stable, episodes can be produced in batches.

Recommended batch structure:

### Batch planning
Define 5–10 episode objectives.

### Script batch
Lock scripts together.

### Asset batch
Identify reusable/new assets.

### Generation batch
Generate scenes systematically.

### Voice batch
Record/generate approved scripts.

### Edit batch
Assemble episodes using the same production templates.

### QA batch
Review consistently across episodes.

Batching improves consistency and reduces context switching.

---

## 43. NEW ASSET REQUEST PROCESS

If an episode needs a new recurring asset:

1. identify why it is needed
2. classify it using the Prop/World system
3. define canonical specification
4. generate reference
5. review
6. approve
7. assign asset ID
8. register in Canonical Asset Registry
9. only then use it in episode scenes

Do not introduce an important recurring asset directly inside a scene without canonicalization.

---

## 44. ONE-OFF ASSETS

A one-off story prop may be created without becoming canonical if it will not recur.

However it must still satisfy:
- Rere visual language
- child safety
- world consistency
- readable interaction
- production QA

If the asset later becomes recurring, promote it into the canonical asset system.

---

## 45. HUMAN REVIEW REQUIREMENT

AI generation does not equal approval.

Human review is mandatory for:
- character identity
- child safety
- educational accuracy
- cultural appropriateness
- visual continuity
- audio quality
- copyright/originality concerns
- final publication

AI is a production accelerator, not the final creative authority.

---

## 46. FINAL EPISODE QA

Before publishing:

### Content
- [ ] objective is clear
- [ ] learning is age-appropriate
- [ ] story is coherent
- [ ] child participation exists

### Character
- [ ] canonical Rere
- [ ] correct outfit
- [ ] correct expressions

### World / Props
- [ ] canonical assets
- [ ] continuity correct

### Camera / Lighting
- [ ] attention is clear
- [ ] emotional language is consistent

### Voice / Audio
- [ ] voice identity stable
- [ ] dialogue intelligible
- [ ] PAUSE preserved
- [ ] audio not overstimulating

### Editing / Motion
- [ ] pacing appropriate
- [ ] no unnecessary effects
- [ ] motion readable

### Packaging
- [ ] title honest
- [ ] thumbnail honest
- [ ] package matches actual episode

### Technical
- [ ] final export checked
- [ ] phone-speaker review completed
- [ ] subtitle/text review completed where applicable

---

## 47. PUBLISHING GATE

An episode becomes **READY TO PUBLISH** only when:

- all critical QA items pass
- canonical assets are confirmed
- final video is reviewed
- title is approved
- thumbnail is approved
- description is approved
- metadata is complete

No episode should publish merely because production is finished.

---

## 48. POST-PUBLISH LEARNING LOOP

After publication, record:

- impressions
- click-through behavior
- average view duration
- retention patterns
- audience feedback
- parent feedback where available
- recurring questions
- production problems
- learning outcomes observed where measurable

Use data to improve the system, not to abandon the core Rere identity after one weak result.

---

## 49. EXPERIMENTATION RULE

Experiment with one major variable at a time where practical.

Possible variables:
- topic
- hook
- title
- thumbnail
- pacing
- episode length
- repetition structure
- CTA placement

Do not change character identity merely to chase performance.

## 51. FINAL PRODUCTION PRINCIPLE

> **Build once. Canonicalize once. Reuse consistently. Improve deliberately.**

The Rere production system should become easier with every episode because each approved asset, template, decision, and lesson becomes part of the reusable production library.

The objective is not merely to make Episode 001.

The objective is to build a system capable of producing **Episode 001 → Episode 010 → Episode 100** without losing what makes Rere Rere.
