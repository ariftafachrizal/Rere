## Initial research-derived principles used in planning

- Early childhood is a high-impact developmental period.
- Play-based and developmentally appropriate experiences are central to early learning.
- Language/literacy, numeracy/cognitive skills, social-emotional development, and self-regulation should be considered interconnected rather than isolated school subjects.
- For young children, interaction/process quality is important; Rere therefore needs to prioritize responsive interaction, questions, pauses, storytelling, repetition, and positive reinforcement rather than passive content delivery.
- Research claims should be validated against primary or high-quality sources before being turned into hard curriculum requirements or public marketing claims.

## Research hygiene

The project should distinguish between:

1. **Evidence-backed learning principles** — suitable for foundational decisions.
2. **Educational practice recommendations** — useful but should be contextualized.
3. **Emerging trends** — interesting for experimentation but not automatically proven.
4. **Marketing/SEO claims** — should not be treated as scientific evidence.

Before publishing strong claims about child development, learning outcomes, screen time, language acquisition, or long-term effects, the project should perform a dedicated source audit.
