# FEEL — Three-Year Curriculum Roadmap

**Status:** Approved Working Curriculum Map — 2026-09-12

## Architecture

**3 Years = MAP → 1 Year = JOURNEY → Quarter = CHAPTER → Episode = EXPERIENCE**

This roadmap is a flexible developmental map, not a fixed syllabus or episode quota.

## Year 1 — Recognize & Feel Safe

**Primary progression:** NOTICE → NAME → REGULATE WITH SUPPORT

### Journey priorities
- familiar emotional situations: happy, sad, angry/frustrated, scared, surprised, calm;
- recognize expressions, tone, body cues, and simple situations;
- predictable co-regulation with Rere/adult/character;
- simple choices: stay, pause, ask for help, move, try again;
- early social routines: greeting, short waiting, simple turn-taking;
- notice when another character needs help;
- establish emotional safety and acceptance of home language.

### Experience families
- character stories with one clear emotional event;
- songs/routines for naming feelings;
- pretend play around everyday frustrations;
- simple “what happened?” and “how does Bunny feel?” invitations;
- adult-supported calming/comfort scenes;
- tiny turn-taking games.

## Year 2 — Practice Together

**Primary progression:** REGULATE WITH SUPPORT → INTERACT → CARE

### Journey priorities
- connect emotion to cause/context;
- practice supported coping strategies in varied situations;
- request, wait, take turns, join play, and handle small disagreements;
- notice another person's feelings and offer concrete help;
- communicate preferences and boundaries;
- retry after frustration;
- begin simple relationship repair.

### Experience families
- cooperative games;
- toy/resource conflicts;
- role-play asking, waiting, helping;
- stories with two characters' different wants;
- change-one-thing scenarios;
- collaborative pretend play;
- routine challenges such as losing, waiting, or plans changing.

## Year 3 — Choose, Repair & Collaborate

**Primary progression:** INTERACT → CARE → BEGIN TO CHOOSE

### Journey priorities
- describe feelings and needs with increasing specificity;
- choose/adapt safe coping responses;
- consider another person's needs without demanding perfect perspective-taking;
- negotiate simple choices and shared plans;
- participate in conflict repair;
- tolerate flexible outcomes and changing plans;
- collaborate toward shared goals;
- transfer emotional/social strategies to new contexts.

### Experience families
- multi-step cooperative stories;
- shared problem-solving play;
- negotiation over roles/resources;
- stories with competing needs;
- repair-and-retry scenarios;
- open-ended role-play where children can choose different safe responses.

## Curriculum Strands Across Three Years

| Strand | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional recognition | notice familiar cues | name + connect to situation | describe/compare in context |
| Regulation | co-regulate | practice supported strategies | choose/adapt strategies |
| Social interaction | early turns | short cooperation | sustained cooperation/negotiation |
| Empathy | notice | care/help | consider needs and adapt |
| Self-awareness | preferences/needs/boundaries | communicate them | reflect and adapt |
| Frustration/flexibility | recover with support | retry/change approach | handle variation with agency |
| Repair | adult-supported | participate | initiate simple repair |

## Spiral Rule

The same functional themes should recur across years:

**feelings → waiting → asking → boundaries → conflict → help → care → repair → collaboration**

What changes is not merely vocabulary. Complexity grows through:

**context + interaction + agency + flexibility + transfer**

## Episode Mapping

Every FEEL episode should define:

1. Primary Learning Intention;
2. primary FEEL Core Area;
3. developmental level/year reference;
4. situation/context;
5. child participation opportunity;
6. safe response options;
7. optional supporting pillars;
8. play mechanism;
9. repetition/variation opportunity;
10. quality-gate checks.

## Six-Month Production Implication

For the initial 2–4 audience, prioritize high-frequency real-life functions over rare emotional vocabulary. A small reusable set of emotions, social functions, and coping choices should recur across many stories and play contexts.

Do not create a separate episode merely because a new emotion word exists. Create an episode when the child has a meaningful situation in which recognizing, expressing, regulating, interacting, caring, or choosing is useful.

## Success Definition

A strong FEEL curriculum should make children increasingly able to:

> **notice what is happening inside and around them, understand that feelings are meaningful, seek or accept support, interact safely with others, care about others, and gradually choose what to do next.**
