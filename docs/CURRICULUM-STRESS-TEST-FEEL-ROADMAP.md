## Key Findings

### 1. No structural gap
The progression handles recognition, regulation, interaction, empathy, agency, flexibility, and repair without requiring a sixth area.

### 2. Agency is preserved
The roadmap does not equate success with obedience, sharing on command, forced apologies, or emotional suppression.

### 3. Co-regulation remains developmentally central
The roadmap correctly allows adult/character support to remain necessary, especially in earlier experiences.

### 4. FEEL/SPEAK boundary is stable
Language may be used heavily, but FEEL remains primary when the intention is understanding emotion/social experience. SPEAK becomes primary when communication itself is the learning goal.

### 5. FEEL/CREATE boundary is stable
Drawing, music, storytelling, and role-play can be FEEL media. CREATE becomes primary when the intention is creative expression or creation.

### 6. FEEL/LIVE boundary is stable
Rules, safety, routines, responsibility, and habits can support FEEL. FEEL is primary when the intention is emotional/social understanding rather than rule acquisition.

## Stress-Test Verdict

**PASS — no structural change required.**

The FEEL framework and roadmap are sufficiently broad for realistic ages 2–4 experiences and can scale toward age 5 through increasing context, agency, interaction, flexibility, and transfer.

## Guardrail

Future episodes should be rejected or redesigned if they make emotional correctness, obedience, forced empathy, forced apology, English performance, or emotional suppression the primary measure of success.
