# Rere — SPEAK Three-Year Curriculum Roadmap

**Status:** Approved Working Roadmap — 2026-09-12

## Purpose

This document turns the approved SPEAK developmental progression into a three-year curriculum map. It is a developmental reference, not a rigid syllabus, age gate, or episode checklist.

> **Stable architecture, flexible curriculum.**

## Developmental Spine

**NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT → COMMUNICATE**

### Year 1 — Build Communication Foundation

**Dominant progression:** NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND

Primary goal: help children experience that language carries meaning and enables participation.

Focus:
- attend to familiar sounds and words;
- understand simple instructions, questions, and descriptions with contextual support;
- recognize and use familiar functional vocabulary;
- participate through gesture, action, pointing, choice, home language, English, or mixed language;
- begin predictable turn-taking;
- identify characters/actions in simple stories;
- enjoy repetition, songs, rhythm, and sound play;
- receive safe, meaningful English exposure.

Design emphasis:
- concrete and familiar contexts;
- predictable routines;
- visual/action support;
- short language models;
- generous processing time;
- verbal production never forced.

### Year 2 — Use Language with Purpose

**Dominant progression:** RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT

Primary goal: help children use language to accomplish real purposes and participate in short exchanges.

Focus:
- understand descriptions, questions, and simple story events;
- expand useful vocabulary through reuse, not vocabulary dumping;
- request, refuse, choose, describe, comment, ask for help, and express preference;
- participate in short back-and-forth exchanges;
- ask and answer simple questions in meaningful contexts;
- sequence and retell familiar events;
- play with rhyme, rhythm, syllables, and sound differences;
- use familiar English words and phrases alongside the home language.

Design emphasis:
- language as a tool for action;
- more child choice and initiation;
- variation of familiar language across contexts;
- cooperative play and shared tasks;
- less reliance on quiz-like prompts.

### Year 3 — Communicate, Narrate & Collaborate

**Dominant progression:** INTERACT → COMMUNICATE

Primary goal: help children use communication to build shared meaning, share experiences and ideas, and accomplish things with others.

Focus:
- follow more complex or multi-step information when developmentally appropriate;
- select and adapt vocabulary to context;
- explain needs, ideas, simple reasons, and experiences;
- sustain and shape conversations;
- narrate, elaborate, retell with detail, and reshape familiar stories;
- negotiate choices and participate in collaborative problem solving;
- use sound/rhythm play with greater flexibility;
- generalize useful English across new but meaningful contexts.

Design emphasis:
- sustained interaction;
- greater child agency;
- less predictable situations with enough scaffolding;
- explanation and shared problem solving;
- transfer rather than performance.

## Strand Roadmap

| Learning strand | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening & Comprehension | familiar words, simple instructions, simple questions | descriptions, questions, story events | multi-step information, simple implied meaning where appropriate |
| Functional Vocabulary | hear, recognize, name useful words | combine and reuse words | select and adapt words by context |
| Functional Communication | request, choose, refuse, respond | describe, ask, help, comment, express preference | explain, negotiate, collaborate |
| Conversation & Social Communication | take a turn, respond | maintain a short exchange | sustain and shape interaction |
| Story & Narrative | identify character/action/event | sequence and retell | elaborate, explain, reshape and share |
| Sound, Rhythm & Phonological Awareness | notice and imitate | distinguish and play with rhyme/rhythm | manipulate and vary simple sound patterns |
| Multilingual & English Communication | contextual exposure and comprehension | functional words/phrases | flexible use and transfer |

## English Progression

English follows a parallel pathway without replacing the broader communication progression:

**HEAR → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → USE → GENERALIZE**

Rules:
1. Communication remains the primary goal.
2. English is an important language pathway, not the definition of SPEAK.
3. Indonesian/home-language responses remain valid participation.
4. Mixed-language responses are acceptable when meaningful.
5. Visuals, actions, gesture, repetition, and context support comprehension.
6. Imitation is a technique, not the endpoint.
7. English-only performance is never the sole measure of learning.
8. Full-English formats may be used as advanced experiences when context is predictable and comprehensible.

## Complexity Model

Progression should increase through four dimensions rather than vocabulary volume alone.

### 1. Context complexity

**Concrete/familiar → varied → less predictable → new context**

### 2. Response complexity

**Action/gesture → choice → word → phrase → connected response**

### 3. Interaction complexity

**Single turn → turn-taking → short exchange → sustained exchange → collaborative interaction**

### 4. Language flexibility

**Model → imitate → adapt → initiate → transfer**

These dimensions can progress at different rates. A child does not need to advance in all four simultaneously.

## Spiral Curriculum Principle

Important communication functions should return repeatedly across the three years with increasing agency and complexity.

Example — asking for help:

- Year 1: recognize/respond to “help”; request help with gesture or word.
- Year 2: use a functional phrase such as “Help me, please.”
- Year 3: explain what help is needed and negotiate assistance.

Example — describing:

- Year 1: “red”, “big”.
- Year 2: “It is a big red ball.”
- Year 3: compare objects and use descriptions to solve a shared task.

Example — story:

- Year 1: identify character and action.
- Year 2: sequence and retell familiar events.
- Year 3: add detail, explain, reshape, and share a story.

## Episode Mapping

The roadmap does not generate episodes directly. Each episode should be derived from:

**Primary Pillar → Primary Learning Intention → Developmental Level → Learning Strand → Play/Interaction Context → Language Target → Supporting Pillars/Capabilities**

Every episode should preserve:
- one clear primary communication intention;
- real communicative purpose;
- meaningful child participation;
- processing time;
- controlled language load;
- repetition with variation;
- safe responses including nonverbal and home-language responses;
- opportunity for transfer where appropriate.

## What Progress Does NOT Mean

Progress is not defined as:
- memorizing more vocabulary;
- speaking only English;
- always producing longer sentences;
- perfect pronunciation or grammar;
- answering every question correctly;
- reducing the use of Bahasa Indonesia/home language;
- performing on command.

Instead:

> **Progress = increasing ability to understand and use communication to participate, express meaning, interact, and accomplish things with others.**

## Relationship to Other Pillars

SPEAK remains primary when communication is the learning intention, even when other domains are naturally involved.

Examples:
- understand a spoken instruction to find a red ball → SPEAK;
- sort balls by color → DISCOVER;
- ask for a turn → SPEAK;
- recognize and respond to a friend's emotion → FEEL;
- follow a shared safety/routine rule → LIVE;
- invent a new Bunny story → CREATE.

> **Activity does not determine the pillar. Learning intention determines the pillar.**

## Curriculum Flexibility

The three-year roadmap is a map, not a fixed sequence of episodes. Theme order, repetition, depth, and format may change based on:
- observed child development;
- audience response and engagement;
- production learning;
- quality review;
- new evidence or research;
- safety considerations;
- the child's changing interests.

Age is a developmental reference, not a cutoff.

## Quality Gate

A roadmap-aligned SPEAK episode should pass the SPEAK Learning Standard before publication. In particular, it must not turn communication into a test, force verbal production, overload vocabulary, or treat English-only performance as the learning outcome.
