# Rere Learning Framework — SPEAK / Communication & English

**Status:** Approved Working Framework — 2026-09-12

## Identity

> **SPEAK — Communicate with the World**

SPEAK adalah Core Pillar Rere yang membantu anak membangun kemampuan untuk **memahami, mengekspresikan, berinteraksi, dan berbagi makna dengan orang lain**.

English adalah bagian penting dari SPEAK untuk audience Rere di Indonesia, tetapi **SPEAK bukan kursus bahasa Inggris**. Tujuan utamanya adalah komunikasi; English menjadi additional language exposure yang dibangun secara natural melalui permainan, cerita, lagu, rutinitas, aktivitas, dan interaksi.

---

## Purpose

SPEAK membantu anak usia dini untuk:

- memahami bahasa yang digunakan dalam konteks bermakna;
- membangun keberanian dan kemampuan untuk merespons;
- menggunakan kata dan frasa untuk tujuan nyata;
- mengambil bagian dalam percakapan dan interaksi sederhana;
- menceritakan pengalaman dan ide secara bertahap;
- menikmati bunyi, ritme, cerita, dan permainan bahasa;
- membangun fondasi komunikasi multilingual tanpa merendahkan home language.

Untuk Rere, keberhasilan SPEAK bukan hanya **berapa banyak vocabulary yang dapat diucapkan anak**, tetapi apakah anak semakin mampu **memahami dan menggunakan bahasa untuk berhubungan dengan dunia**.

---

# Developmental Progression

## CORE PROGRESSION

**NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT → COMMUNICATE**

Progression ini menggambarkan perkembangan kemampuan komunikasi, bukan tahapan umur yang kaku.

### 1. NOTICE — “Aku mendengar dan memperhatikan.”

Anak:
- memperhatikan suara dan ucapan;
- mengenali kata/frasa yang sering muncul;
- menghubungkan kata dengan objek, tindakan, ekspresi, atau konteks;
- menikmati lagu, rhyme, dan pola bunyi;
- mengenali nama diri, orang, dan benda familiar.

Rere response:
- gunakan kata yang konsisten;
- dukung dengan visual, gesture, ekspresi, dan tindakan;
- jangan menuntut anak mengulang.

### 2. UNDERSTAND — “Aku tahu maksudnya.”

Anak mulai:
- mengikuti instruksi sederhana;
- memahami pertanyaan sederhana;
- memilih benda berdasarkan kata/deskripsi;
- memahami hubungan kata dengan kejadian dalam cerita atau permainan;
- menunjukkan pemahaman tanpa harus berbicara.

Prinsip penting:
> **Comprehension may come before production.**

Anak tidak harus langsung mengucapkan kata yang sudah mulai dipahaminya.

### 3. RESPOND — “Aku bisa menjawab atau melakukan sesuatu.”

Respons dapat berupa:
- menunjuk;
- mengambil/memindahkan benda;
- melakukan tindakan;
- gesture;
- memilih;
- satu kata;
- suara;
- kombinasi home language + English;
- frasa pendek.

SPEAK menghargai **respons yang bermakna**, bukan hanya jawaban verbal yang sempurna.

### 4. EXPRESS — “Aku bisa mengatakan sesuatu.”

Anak mulai menggunakan bahasa untuk:
- meminta;
- menolak;
- menyebutkan;
- mendeskripsikan;
- menyatakan suka/tidak suka;
- meminta bantuan;
- memberi informasi sederhana;
- menceritakan pengalaman.

### 5. INTERACT — “Aku bisa menggunakan bahasa bersama orang lain.”

Anak mulai:
- bergiliran dalam percakapan;
- merespons kontribusi orang lain;
- mengajukan atau menjawab pertanyaan sederhana;
- menggunakan bahasa sosial;
- mengikuti percakapan singkat;
- menggunakan bahasa dalam cooperative play.

### 6. COMMUNICATE — “Aku menggunakan bahasa untuk mencapai sesuatu bersama orang lain.”

Anak mulai menggunakan bahasa untuk:
- menyampaikan ide;
- menjelaskan kebutuhan;
- mempertahankan interaksi beberapa giliran;
- menceritakan urutan kejadian sederhana;
- memecahkan masalah bersama;
- memilih dan menegosiasikan pilihan sederhana;
- menyesuaikan komunikasi dengan situasi secara bertahap.

### Developmental interpretation

| Phase | Dominant communication growth | Typical experience |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | NOTICE | hear, watch, recognize |
| 2 | UNDERSTAND | follow, identify, choose |
| 3 | RESPOND | point, act, gesture, answer |
| 4 | EXPRESS | name, request, describe |
| 5 | INTERACT | take turns, respond, ask |
| 6 | COMMUNICATE | share ideas, narrate, solve together |

Age is a reference, not a gate. A child may show different levels across listening, speaking, social interaction, and English exposure.

# Learning Strands

## 1. Listening & Comprehension

Primary question:
> **Can the child understand meaning from language in context?**

Includes:
- familiar words;
- simple instructions;
- questions;
- descriptions;
- story events;
- sequence;
- listening for key information;
- understanding through visual/contextual clues.

Progression:
**Hear → Recognize → Understand → Follow → Interpret simple meaning**

---

## 2. Functional Vocabulary

Vocabulary is selected because it is **useful, meaningful, visualizable, and reusable**.

Priority categories:
- people/family;
- body;
- food/drink;
- toys;
- animals;
- objects around home;
- actions;
- colors/shapes/size;
- locations;
- nature/environment;
- feelings;
- social words;
- routine words.

Progression:
**Hear → Recognize → Name → Use → Reuse in new context**

Vocabulary should be learned as part of a semantic/context network, not isolated lists.

---

## 3. Functional Phrases & Communicative Functions

SPEAK teaches language because children **need language to do something**.

Key functions:
- greeting;
- requesting;
- refusing;
- choosing;
- asking for help;
- answering;
- commenting;
- describing;
- inviting;
- thanking;
- apologizing/repairing when appropriate;
- expressing preference;
- participating in play.

Examples:
- “I want water.”
- “Help me, please.”
- “My turn.”
- “Can I have it?”
- “I like this.”
- “Let’s play.”
- “It’s big.”
- “Where is Bunny?”

Progression:
**Model → Complete/imitate → Adapt → Initiate → Use spontaneously**

---

## 4. Conversation & Social Communication

Focus:
- turn-taking;
- responding to another person;
- initiating interaction;
- maintaining a short exchange;
- asking simple questions;
- listening to another person's contribution;
- using social language in play.

Important principle:
> **A conversation is not a quiz.**

Rere should not continuously ask questions and immediately provide the answer. Give the child space to respond, then respond meaningfully to the child's contribution.

Preferred interaction loop:

**Rere models/asks → PAUSE → child responds/acts → Rere acknowledges → extend → child responds again**

---

## 5. Story & Narrative Communication

Story is a powerful vehicle for SPEAK because it naturally combines listening, vocabulary, sequencing, comprehension, and expression.

Includes:
- character identification;
- event recognition;
- prediction;
- simple sequencing;
- retelling;
- describing what happened;
- personal experience sharing;
- connecting story to the child's world.

Progression:
**Listen → Identify → Sequence → Retell → Add detail → Tell/reshape**

Boundary with CREATE:
- **SPEAK:** primary intention is understanding/using language through story.
- **CREATE:** primary intention is inventing, changing, or expressing a story as a creative act.

---

## 6. Sound, Rhythm & Phonological Awareness

For ages 2–4, focus on playful awareness rather than formal phonics drilling.

Includes:
- noticing environmental sounds;
- animal/object sounds;
- rhyme;
- repetition;
- syllable/rhythm awareness;
- alliteration-like sound play;
- listening for differences/similarities in sounds;
- songs, chants, and playful word patterns.

Progression:
**Notice → Imitate → Distinguish → Play → Manipulate simple sound patterns**

Formal letter-sound instruction is not required in every SPEAK episode.

---

## 7. Multilingual & English Communication

English is a **target exposure language**, not a replacement for Bahasa Indonesia.

Working direction for Indonesian audience:

> **Indonesian context + meaningful English exposure + responsive interaction**

English can be introduced through:
- repeated contextual phrases;
- visual/action pairing;
- songs and rhythm;
- stories;
- routines;
- play commands;
- functional communication.

A child may respond in:
- Indonesian;
- English;
- a mixture;
- gesture/action;
- another familiar communication form.

Rere should respond to meaning first, then model useful English naturally when appropriate.

This is consistent with NAEYC guidance emphasizing home language as a resource and responsive multilingual interaction rather than treating English-only use as the default. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn0search2

# English Exposure Standard

## The Rere English Ladder

**HEAR → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → USE → GENERALIZE**

English learning should move from contextual recognition toward meaningful use.

### HEAR
English appears consistently and naturally.

### UNDERSTAND
Visuals, actions, story context, and repetition make meaning accessible.

### RESPOND
The child can respond with action, gesture, home language, English, or a combination.

### USE
The child begins using familiar English words/fragments in meaningful situations.

### GENERALIZE
The child encounters the same language function in new contexts.

This replaces the older narrower progression **Hear & Recognize → Repeat & Imitate → Respond → Use** as the primary Rere English progression. Imitation remains a technique inside the progression, not a developmental endpoint.

---

# Language Mix Standard

No fixed Indonesian:English percentage is required at this stage.

Instead, Rere uses a **comprehension-first language design**:

1. Anchor meaning in context.
2. Introduce English consistently.
3. Support English with visual/action/gesture.
4. Repeat the same useful language across varied situations.
5. Use Indonesian when necessary to preserve meaning, emotional safety, or narrative flow.
6. Do not mechanically translate every sentence.
7. Never treat English-only performance as the measure of learning.

### Full-English content

Full-English episodes are allowed as an **advanced format**, not a default requirement.

They should be used when:
- the visual context is sufficiently self-explanatory;
- language is highly predictable;
- repetition is strong;
- actions communicate meaning;
- the episode remains enjoyable without translation.

Full-English should not become a purity rule.

---

# Episode Language Standard

Every SPEAK episode should define:

- **Primary communication intention:** one sentence;
- **Target language function:** e.g. request, describe, greet, choose;
- **Core vocabulary:** usually a small reusable set, not a long list;
- **Core phrase/model:** one or a few high-value expressions;
- **Context:** why the child would use/understand the language;
- **Child response opportunity:** action, choice, gesture, word, or phrase;
- **Variation:** where the same language appears again in a new context;
- **Supporting pillars:** only when naturally useful.

### Vocabulary rule

Rere should optimize for **depth and reuse before breadth**.

There is no universal fixed vocabulary quota per episode. The production team should choose the smallest useful set that allows the child to participate meaningfully.

---

# Rere Interaction Standard

The standard interaction pattern is:

**MODEL → INVITE → PAUSE → RECEIVE → RESPOND → EXTEND → REPEAT WITH VARIATION**

### MODEL
Rere gives a clear language example.

### INVITE
Rere creates a reason for the child to respond.

### PAUSE
Allow processing time. Do not answer immediately.

### RECEIVE
Accept the child's actual contribution, including nonverbal or home-language responses.

### RESPOND
Rere acknowledges meaning rather than only judging correctness.

### EXTEND
Add a slightly richer model when appropriate.

### REPEAT WITH VARIATION
Use the same language in a new but related context.

This aligns with evidence-informed early-childhood practice emphasizing responsive conversations, active child participation, joint activities, and meaningful context. citeturn0search1turn0search13

# Learning Standard / Quality Bar

A SPEAK episode is considered **learning-standard ready** when:

### 1. Communication intention is clear
There is one primary communicative learning intention.

### 2. Language has a real purpose
The child needs or can use the language to do something meaningful.

### 3. Comprehension is supported
The child can infer meaning from context, visual, gesture, action, story, or repetition.

### 4. Child participation is real
The episode creates genuine opportunities to act, choose, respond, speak, or otherwise communicate.

### 5. There is processing time
The child is not expected to respond instantly.

### 6. Production is not forced
Listening/understanding counts as legitimate participation.

### 7. Home language is respected
Indonesian or another home language is not portrayed as a problem or failure.

### 8. Repetition has variation
Important language reappears without becoming mechanical drilling.

### 9. Language load is controlled
The episode does not introduce too many new words/functions at once.

### 10. Errors are safe
Pronunciation or grammar mistakes are modeled/recast naturally rather than shamed.

### 11. The interaction is responsive
Rere reacts to the child's possible contribution rather than following a rigid quiz script.

### 12. Transfer is possible
The child can encounter the same word/function in another everyday context.

### 13. Supporting domains remain subordinate
DISCOVER, FEEL, LIVE, and CREATE may support the experience, but should not blur the primary SPEAK intention.

### 14. Play remains visible
The episode feels like an experience or interaction, not a classroom worksheet translated into video.

# Anti-Patterns

Rere should avoid:

- vocabulary flashcard dumping;
- “repeat after me” for every line;
- immediate answer after every question;
- correcting every pronunciation error;
- English-only as ideology;
- translation of every sentence;
- grammar lectures;
- long passive monologues;
- unrealistic “speak English fluently” expectations;
- testing children repeatedly;
- treating silence as failure;
- using praise only for correct English;
- excessive new vocabulary;
- forcing children to perform for adults;
- confusing memorization with communication.

# Cross-Pillar Boundaries

| Experience | Primary Pillar | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Find the red ball after hearing “red ball” | SPEAK | comprehension of language |
| Sort balls by color | DISCOVER | classification is primary |
| Ask for a turn | SPEAK | functional social communication |
| Understand that a friend is upset | FEEL | emotional understanding |
| Wait for a turn because of a shared rule | LIVE | social/routine responsibility |
| Invent a song using “red, blue” | CREATE | creative expression |
| Tell a new story about Bunny | CREATE | narrative creation |
| Retell what Bunny did | SPEAK | narrative language |

Rule remains:
> **Activity does not determine the pillar. Learning intention determines the pillar.**

# Example Episode Standard

## “Rere Wants the Red Cup”

**Primary Pillar:** SPEAK

**Primary Learning Intention:** Child experiences and begins to use language for requesting an object.

**Core language:**
- cup
- red
- want
- please
- thank you

**Core function:** requesting

**Flow:**

1. Rere sees several cups.
2. Rere models: “I want the red cup.”
3. Rere invites the child to choose.
4. Pause.
5. Child may point, say “red,” say “cup,” say “mau,” say “I want,” or simply act.
6. Rere responds to meaning: “Yes! The red cup. You want the red cup.”
7. Repeat in another context.
8. End with natural social language: “Thank you!”

Supporting capabilities:
- choice/agency;
- attention;
- turn-taking.

Possible supporting pillars:
- DISCOVER: color recognition;
- LIVE: polite social routine.

But SPEAK remains primary because the learning intention is communication.

# Evidence-Informed Notes

The framework is informed by early-childhood guidance emphasizing:

- home language as an asset rather than an obstacle;
- responsive, reciprocal conversation;
- meaningful context;
- active participation rather than passive exposure;
- joint activities, play, storytelling, and songs as natural language contexts;
- visual, object, body-language, and contextual supports for children learning an additional language;
- recognition that children may understand language before producing it.

NAEYC specifically describes home-language use and translanguaging as resources for meaning-making and recommends responsive interaction that values children's contributions. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn0search2

# Current Decisions

1. **SPEAK is a Core Pillar** under the final five-pillar architecture.
2. SPEAK's identity is **Communicate with the World**, not “English class.”
3. Primary developmental progression is:
   **NOTICE → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → EXPRESS → INTERACT → COMMUNICATE**
4. English progression is:
   **HEAR → UNDERSTAND → RESPOND → USE → GENERALIZE**
5. English exposure uses Indonesian context + meaningful English exposure + responsive interaction.
6. No fixed Indonesian:English percentage is required yet.
7. Full-English episodes are an optional advanced format, not a default.
8. Vocabulary is selected for usefulness, context, depth, and reuse rather than a fixed quota.
9. Child communication may be verbal, nonverbal, Indonesian, English, or mixed.
10. Imitation is a useful learning technique but is not treated as the endpoint of communication development.
11. Learning quality is judged by meaningful communication opportunities, not English performance alone.
