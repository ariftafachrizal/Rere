# RERE — AUDIO IDENTITY BIBLE

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Production Standard  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Project:** YouTube Rere  
**Primary Audience:** Ages 2–4 (first 6 months)  
**Language:** Indonesian primary; English bilingual support

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the canonical audio identity for the Rere universe.

Audio must make Rere feel:
- warm
- safe
- cheerful
- playful
- curious
- emotionally clear
- educational without feeling like a classroom lecture

The audio world must support the learning philosophy:

> **PLAY → EXPERIENCE → IMITATE → LEARN**

Audio is not decoration. Audio is **attention guidance, emotional safety, rhythm, and memory support**.

---

## 2. CORE AUDIO PRINCIPLE

> **Rere can sound excited, curious, surprised, calm, or proud — but Rere must always sound like Rere.**

Music, voice, ambience, and sound effects may change according to the story, but the overall sonic identity must remain recognizable.

The audio should feel like a gentle, playful children's world rather than an overstimulating children's channel.

---

## 3. AUDIO HIERARCHY

Every scene follows this priority:

1. **Rere voice / dialogue** — highest priority
2. **Learning-object or interaction SFX**
3. **Narrative / emotional music**
4. **Environmental ambience**
5. **Decorative sonic details**

When elements compete, lower-priority layers must be reduced rather than making Rere louder and louder.

---

## 4. RERE VOICE IDENTITY

### 4.1 Character

Rere is a preschool learning companion, not a lecturer.

Voice qualities:
- childlike but intelligible
- warm
- friendly
- expressive
- curious
- gentle
- confident without sounding bossy
- positive
- natural

### 4.2 Language

Primary language: **Bahasa Indonesia**.

English may be introduced when pedagogically useful, especially for:
- simple vocabulary
- songs
- repeated labels
- bilingual learning moments

Do not switch languages merely for decoration.

### 4.3 Speech Rhythm

For ages 2–4:
- short sentences
- clear articulation
- deliberate keyword emphasis
- natural micro-pauses
- enough time for children to process
- avoid rapid-fire dialogue

Rere should sound as though she is talking **with** a child, not performing **at** a child.

### 4.4 Canonical Opening

Every standard episode begins with:

> **“Hai teman-teman, aku Rere, main dan belajar bersama Rere, yuk!”**

Do not casually rewrite this line. Any intentional change is a versioned creative decision.

### 4.5 Canonical Closing

Use the established closing sequence:

- “Hebat sekali hari ini!”
- “Terima kasih sudah belajar bersama Rere!”
- both hands form heart
- “Belajar... Bermain... Berbagi... Bersama Rere!”
- “Sampai jumpa, teman-teman! Dadah!”

End card:

> **BELAJAR • BERMAIN • BERBAGI**  
> **BERSAMA RERE!**

---

## 5. INTERACTION AUDIO PATTERN

The canonical interaction pattern is:

**Rere → bertanya → PAUSE → anak menjawab/bertindak → Rere merespons**

### PAUSE

The pause is a designed learning component, not empty space.

During a question:
- music should simplify or soften
- unnecessary SFX should stop
- ambience may remain very low
- voice should finish clearly
- leave sufficient processing time before the answer/reward

Do not fill every pause with sound.

### Response

After the pause, Rere should acknowledge participation even when the child cannot answer.

Preferred emotional direction:
- “Yuk, kita lihat!”
- “Betul!”
- “Hampir benar!”
- “Ayo coba lagi!”
- “Hebat, kamu mencoba!”

Avoid shame, sarcasm, or exaggerated failure sounds.

---

## 6. RERE SONIC SIGNATURE

Rere should have a small recurring sonic identity that can appear in:
- opening
- closing
- selected transitions
- channel branding

The signature should be:
- short
- melodic
- gentle
- memorable
- easy to recognize after repeated exposure
- compatible with both regular and Muslimah Rere

It should **not** become an intrusive sound effect after every sentence.

Recommended concept:

> **3–5 note upward, playful, warm motif**

The exact melody should be versioned and stored as a canonical audio asset once approved.

Suggested asset ID:

`RERE-AUDIO-SONIC-LOGO-01`

---

## 7. OPENING MUSIC

Opening music establishes the Rere universe immediately.

Characteristics:
- cheerful
- welcoming
- light
- short
- recognizable
- not too busy

The opening should leave enough sonic space for the canonical spoken opening.

Suggested asset ID:

`RERE-AUDIO-OPENING-THEME-01`

Opening sequence:

**Sonic signature → visual identity → canonical spoken opening → episode hook**

The spoken opening remains more important than the music.

---

## 8. CLOSING MUSIC

Closing music should communicate:
- accomplishment
- warmth
- gratitude
- gentle goodbye

It should not feel like an abrupt ending or a high-energy finale.

Suggested asset ID:

`RERE-AUDIO-CLOSING-THEME-01`

The closing theme may reuse the sonic signature so the episode ends with recognizable Rere branding.

---

## 9. MUSIC SYSTEM

Music is categorized by narrative function rather than by episode.

### 9.1 Discovery Music

Use when Rere discovers something new.

Feel:
- curious
- light
- gently playful

### 9.2 Learning Music

Use while teaching or repeating concepts.

Feel:
- simple
- rhythmic
- predictable
- supportive

Music must never compete with the learning word.

### 9.3 Imagination Music

Use for imaginative worlds or pretend play.

Feel:
- magical
- spacious
- playful
- slightly wonder-filled

### 9.4 Emotional Music

Use for:
- kindness
- empathy
- friendship
- confidence
- calming moments

Feel:
- warm
- soft
- emotionally readable

### 9.5 Action Music

Use for movement, searching, sorting, or playful activity.

Keep tempo appropriate for preschool attention and motor activity. Avoid relentless intensity.

---

## 10. MUSIC RULES FOR AGES 2–4

Prefer:
- simple melodies
- repetition
- clear rhythmic patterns
- predictable structure
- small instrumental palettes
- gentle dynamic changes

Avoid:
- aggressive drops
- constant rapid percussion
- dense arrangements
- sudden extreme volume changes
- distracting melodic counter-lines during speech
- excessively fast transitions

The goal is **engagement without overstimulation**.

---

## 11. SOUND EFFECT SYSTEM

SFX should make actions understandable and satisfying.

Core categories:

### 11.1 Interaction SFX
- pick up
- put down
- open
- close
- touch
- move
- sort
- match
- count
- turn page

### 11.2 Learning SFX
- correct answer
- discovery
- reveal
- match confirmation
- count confirmation

### 11.3 Transition SFX
- scene change
- magical movement
- playful wipe
- chapter transition

### 11.4 Emotional SFX
- gentle surprise
- tiny sparkle
- soft delight
- calming breath-like texture

SFX should reinforce what the child can already see. They should not create confusing actions that are absent visually.

---

## 12. REWARD SOUND

Correct answers and successful actions may receive a short reward cue.

Reward sound should communicate:
- “yes”
- “you did it”
- “success”

Recommended character:
- short
- bright
- warm
- musically related to Rere's sonic signature

Avoid loud applause, sirens, explosions, or arcade-style reward sounds.

Suggested asset ID:

`RERE-AUDIO-REWARD-01`

---

## 13. PAUSE / THINKING SOUND

A question does **not** require a continuous “thinking” sound.

Default:
- silence or very soft ambience

Optional cue:
- a subtle, short, gentle tonal bed before the pause

Never use a ticking clock or stressful countdown unless a future episode explicitly requires it and it is developmentally appropriate.

Suggested asset ID:

`RERE-AUDIO-PAUSE-CUE-01`

---

## 14. AMBIENCE

Every environment may have a low-level sonic bed.

Examples:

| World | Ambient direction |
|---|---|
| Playroom | soft indoor room tone, distant playful toy texture |
| Bedroom | calm indoor room tone, subtle cozy texture |
| Kitchen | gentle home ambience, soft utensil/room sounds |
| Garden | birds, leaves, light outdoor air |
| Classroom | soft room tone, distant child-safe activity |
| Outdoor Play | light wind, distant play ambience |
| Community | gentle public-space ambience without crowd harshness |
| Market | soft market texture, controlled voices |
| Nature | birds, leaves, water where appropriate |
| Imagination | designed magical atmosphere, sparse and spacious |

Ambience must remain subordinate to Rere's voice.

---

## 15. AUDIO DYNAMICS / MIXING

### Priority rule

When Rere speaks:
- dialogue is intelligible first
- music ducks as needed
- important SFX may remain but must not mask speech
- ambience stays low

### Dynamic behavior

Avoid:
- sudden loud peaks
- extreme compression
- aggressive limiting
- excessive bass
- harsh high frequencies

Preserve a comfortable listening experience for children and parents.

Exact technical loudness targets should be standardized during post-production testing against the final platform delivery specification.

---

## 16. VOICE + MUSIC + SFX INTERACTION

### Teaching moment

**Voice > learning SFX > music > ambience**

### Story moment

**Voice > music > relevant SFX > ambience**

### Action moment

**Voice > action SFX/music > ambience**

### Emotional moment

**Voice > emotional music > subtle ambience/SFX**

### PAUSE moment

**Silence / ambience > minimal cue > answer/reward**

---

## 17. AUDIO TRANSITIONS

Transitions should feel continuous rather than like separate videos stitched together.

Preferred:
- short musical tails
- gentle tonal swells
- soft sparkle
- natural room-to-room transition
- Rere sonic motif used selectively

Avoid:
- loud whooshes
- impact hits
- cinematic booms
- aggressive risers
- excessive transition frequency

---

## 18. EMOTIONAL AUDIO MODES

### HAPPY
Bright, light, bouncy, warm.

### CURIOUS
Sparse, playful, small upward motifs.

### SURPRISED
Short tonal accent, then quickly return to normal.

### CALM
Slow, spacious, soft dynamics.

### PROUD / ACHIEVEMENT
Warm upward musical movement, short and satisfying.

### KINDNESS / EMPATHY
Soft melodic support, intimate and non-dramatic.

### IMAGINATION
Magical texture with space and restraint.

---

## 19. MUSIC TEMPO GUIDANCE

Tempo is a creative parameter, not a fixed identity lock.

General direction:
- conversation: no rhythmic pressure
- learning: moderate and predictable
- playful action: moderately energetic
- calm: slower and spacious
- imagination: flexible, often floating

Do not increase tempo merely to prevent children from losing attention.

---

## 20. AUDIO CONTINUITY

For recurring locations and props, audio should also maintain continuity.

Examples:
- the same recurring bunny should have the same interaction sound language
- the same learning book should have a consistent page-turn character
- the playroom should maintain recognizable ambience
- opening/closing branding should remain stable across episodes

Do not regenerate recurring sounds as unrelated variants without versioning.

---

## 21. REGULAR RERE & MUSLIMAH RERE

Regular Rere and Muslimah Rere share the same audio universe.

They must retain:
- same vocal identity
- same sonic logo
- same opening/closing identity
- same general music language
- same SFX philosophy

Wardrobe changes do not create a new audio character.

---

## 22. AUDIO + LEARNING FRAMEWORK

Audio must reinforce the five learning pillars:

### DISCOVER
Use subtle curiosity cues and reveal sounds.

### SPEAK
Leave clean space for Rere and the child to speak.

### FEEL
Use emotionally legible but restrained music.

### LIVE
Use natural action SFX and real-world ambience.

### CREATE
Use playful construction, drawing, sorting, or making sounds.

The method remains:

> **PLAY → EXPERIENCE → IMITATE → LEARN**

---

## 23. AUDIO + CAMERA

Audio and camera must work together.

When camera moves attention toward a learning object:
- allow a corresponding subtle SFX or musical cue
- do not over-score the movement

When camera enters a PAUSE/question shot:
- reduce audio density
- prioritize voice clarity
- preserve processing space

When camera reveals an answer:
- use a short reveal/reward cue if appropriate

Camera guides visual attention; audio reinforces that attention.

---

## 24. AUDIO + LIGHTING

Audio should support, not contradict, the emotional lighting mode.

Examples:
- warm morning light → light, fresh ambience
- calm bedroom light → soft, quiet texture
- imaginative lighting → sparse magical texture
- emotional warm light → gentle melodic support

Do not use frightening or suspense-heavy audio simply because a scene uses dramatic lighting.

---

## 25. AI AUDIO GENERATION ANCHOR

Use this anchor when generating Rere audio:

> **Canonical Rere audio universe, preschool-safe, warm and playful, Indonesian early-childhood learning, gentle expressive child companion, clear speech, short sentences, natural micro-pauses, simple memorable melodies, restrained sound effects, soft environmental ambience, non-overstimulating dynamics, recognizable Rere sonic signature, consistent with the Rere Character, World, Camera, Lighting, and Voice Bibles.**

### Negative audio prompt

Avoid:
- horror
- suspense-heavy scoring
- aggressive cinematic impacts
- loud explosions
- harsh alarms
- distorted child voice
- adult-sounding voice
- seductive or inappropriate vocal tone
- robotic speech
- excessive autotune artifacts
- rapid-fire dialogue
- constant percussion
- sensory overload
- extreme volume jumps
- arcade/game-show reward sounds
- sadistic/failure sounds
- scary creature sounds
- copyrighted song imitation
- recognizable imitation of another creator's signature audio

---

## 26. COPYRIGHT / ORIGINALITY RULE

Rere's music and sonic identity should be original or properly licensed.

Do not request AI to imitate a specific living artist, copyrighted children's show theme, or another channel's distinctive audio identity.

Use functional descriptors instead:
- playful preschool
- warm acoustic
- gentle marimba-like texture
- soft bells
- simple children's melody
- light hand percussion

The Rere sonic logo must become an original canonical asset.

## 28. VERSIONING

Never overwrite a canonical audio asset without recording a version change.

Example:

`RERE-AUDIO-SONIC-LOGO-01`
`RERE-AUDIO-SONIC-LOGO-02`

Once approved, the active version becomes the canonical production reference.

---

## 29. AUDIO QA CHECKLIST

Before an episode is approved:

### Voice
- [ ] Rere voice remains recognizable
- [ ] Indonesian pronunciation is clear
- [ ] dialogue is understandable for ages 2–4
- [ ] pacing allows processing
- [ ] keywords are emphasized naturally
- [ ] no distracting artifacts

### Music
- [ ] music supports the scene
- [ ] music does not mask speech
- [ ] repetition is purposeful
- [ ] dynamics are gentle
- [ ] no unnecessary overstimulation

### SFX
- [ ] SFX match visible actions
- [ ] SFX are short and clear
- [ ] reward sounds are restrained
- [ ] no frightening or harsh effects

### Ambience
- [ ] environment is recognizable
- [ ] ambience remains subordinate
- [ ] continuity is maintained

### Interaction
- [ ] PAUSE is audible as intentional space
- [ ] child response time is sufficient
- [ ] reward follows the learning action appropriately

### Branding
- [ ] opening audio identity is correct
- [ ] closing audio identity is correct
- [ ] sonic logo version is correct

### Technical
- [ ] no clipping
- [ ] no unwanted noise
- [ ] no sudden level jumps
- [ ] dialogue remains intelligible on phone speakers
- [ ] dialogue remains comfortable on headphones

---

## 30. CANONICAL AUDIO LOCK

Once the following are approved, they become canonical:

- Rere voice identity
- canonical opening voice recording
- canonical closing voice recording
- sonic logo
- opening theme
- closing theme
- core reward cue
- core interaction SFX language
- core ambience language

Future episodes should reuse canonical assets wherever possible.

New sounds may be introduced when the story requires them, but they must remain compatible with the established audio world.

---

## 31. PRODUCTION PRINCIPLE

> **Let children hear what matters, feel what happens, and have enough quiet to participate.**

The strongest Rere audio moment is not necessarily the loudest or busiest one.

Rere's audio identity should make children feel:

**“Aku aman. Aku diajak bermain. Aku boleh mencoba. Aku bisa belajar.”**
